# Saint Shard: player-led investigation roadmap

Approved for phased implementation on 26 October 2026. Reviewed baseline: `5bfcb59`, live and passing full CI. Two source-informed browser campaigns covered 130 visits without observed script/media failures; performances were not auditioned. The existing AAA production program remains separate; this roadmap promises no review score.

## Player promise and review

Build an account of Ward Nine that withstands a challenge. Discover a lead → construct a hypothesis → prepare a plan → act → face a response → revise the case.

The current game already has reconstruction, specialist methods, local opportunities, recovery, consent and character consequences. Investigations still often present predetermined interpretations; hubs offer nine to eleven choices; world consequences largely arrive through authored returns and summaries. Preserve the working scarce-resource recovery and the distinctions between recorded claims, independent corroboration, key authentication and permission.

## Phase 1 — Player-built investigations (two cycles)

Establish a bounded saved case model with native export/import, legacy and held-roll compatibility. Connect acquired sources to claims, identify limits and test the assembled account. An annotation grants neither truth nor consent. A tested case must affect available archive/hearing decisions; weak accounts receive specific challenges and corrected drafts retain previous reviews. Support a bounded recorded account and an independently corroborated one. Exit: two defensible accounts, a challenged weak account, a meaningful correction and actual gameplay decisions, with keyboard/list, narrow enlarged text and text-only access.

## Phase 2 — Plans that change execution (two cycles)

Build an archive preparation desk: objective, approach and contingency. Preview costs and capabilities. Execution responds to the combination: choose exposure or effort, retrieve the source and handle a failure through the selected contingency. Two approaches need different decisions and complications, not just modifiers. Preserve once-only costs, recorded dice and affordable recovery. Viewing a plan never spends, trains, retrieves a source or grants consent. Exit: contrasting complete campaigns with a viable low-resource path. Extend to witness/depot work only after the archive prototype proves useful.

## Phase 3 — Reactive characters and districts (two to three cycles)

Begin with Edda and Kerr: separate what they know, what the runner told them and what they will do. An audit can alter access; a public allegation can trigger a counteroffer, close an opportunity or open another. Prototype one changing district, expressed through work, conversation, art and sound. Reuse saved action windows for pressure; reading, inspection and accessibility controls consume no opportunities. Exit: an identifiable player-caused district change and a character response tied to a specific disclosure or promise.

## Phase 4 — Distinct builds (two cycles)

Expand training/equipment into a small set of techniques with sacrifices and useful combinations. Chrome offers technical investigation, Ghost reshapes access/exposure, Face changes voluntary cooperation/negotiation, and Nerve supports handling/recovery. Preserve 1d10, caps and held results; audit stacking and scarce-resource completion. Exit: four builds solve the same anchor through recognizably different interactions whose consequences persist.

## Phase 5 — Cinematic art and voice direction (two cycles)

Direct complete confession, confrontation, witness-decision and Ward Nine return sequences. Use establishing images, reactions and evidence close-ups purposefully. District variants depict earned states. Compare performances, pauses, intelligibility and music transitions rather than counting tracks. Media accompanies changes in every phase: script lock → actor/transcript binding → recording/retake → trim/mix → matching shot → anatomy/continuity → phone/accessibility. Exit: text, actual actor, audio and artwork agree; silent/text-only/reduced-motion/whole-scene play remain complete.

## Phase 6 — Campaign integration and payoff (two to three cycles)

Replace repetitive steps before increasing length. Connect cases, plans and character knowledge across three acts. Preserve the four original finales; consider optional short aftermath interactions for earned consequences. Validate prepared, scarce, failed, deferred and unfinished campaigns. AI playthroughs remain the development proxy, distinct from later human/device acceptance. Exit: explain how evidence, build and relationships changed the campaign, with save/security/media/accessibility/performance and full browser gates intact.

## First five implementation cycles

1. Case-state model, compatibility and usable evidence board.
2. Challenge/correction driven by assembled sources.
3. Two contrasting archive plans.
4. Playable complications and contingencies.
5. Edda's knowledge-driven responses and first district reaction.

## Delivery and scope

Planning allowance: 12–14 cycles, not a calendar promise; re-estimate after the prototype. First target: a 20–30-minute Ward Nine case, four core sources, two approaches, two reactive characters and several consequential outcomes. Duration is a target, not measured playtime.

The user authorized at least Phases 1 and 2 now, sequentially, publishing after each. Each publication requires source/build/security, focused play/browser checks, unchanged full hosted matrix, exact-commit Vercel readiness and live feature checks. Update README/AGENTS each commit. Preserve original media/story when suitable; new scenes or spoken lines require explicit art and matching voice. Record post-push release evidence separately to avoid endlessly restarting CI for status-only documentation. Never call a partial prototype or local test a completed production phase.
