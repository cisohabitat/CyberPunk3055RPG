# Saint Shard production direction

## Product pillars

1. Memory is a source, a liability, and a thing the player can alter.
2. Every moral decision has a practical consequence and a person affected by it.
3. Failure changes the situation while leaving an authored way forward.
4. Reading remains comfortable on a phone and controllable with keyboard, touch, and optional controller input.

The browser game remains a solo narrative RPG with 1d10 checks. Four original finales retain their identity. New work deepens evidence, opportunities, and aftermath within that format.

## Narrative and systemic standards

The memory bench distinguishes a recorded claim from authenticated documents. A protected roster is not a rescued witness; an export is not an authenticated command chain. Each disposition opens a corresponding task during The Week. A failed check introduces a specific cost or complication: checkpoint negotiation, clinic responsibility, a flagged badge, or an unverified ledger.

Quill talks in concrete prices and favors. Lumen talks in responsibility for patients. Ives asks for issuer, authorization, and record ownership. Sera asks who can speak for a name. Nia sets conditions on use of her account. Edit passages toward those motives, and use metaphors sparingly enough that each voice remains identifiable.

Each origin earns a different practical resource. The earned skill is a single deliberate choice with a visible bonus. Assess balance with observed player choices as well as seeded simulations. Completion simulations cannot establish that a build is fun or fair.

## Visual direction

Use warm, worn skin against cool institutional light. Amber belongs to costs and warnings; cyan to access and inspected evidence; magenta to named speakers and the chapel's pressure. Preserve legible text surfaces over environmental plates. The mobile scene header keeps the current goal visible and places prose below the portrait.

The memory plate uses timestamps, source labels, and explicit claim/fact distinctions. Its meaning must survive high contrast and reduced motion. Environmental movement and transitions should support scene changes without moving reading text. Foreground images have dimensions to avoid layout shifts.

Before producing additional art, establish a character model sheet, environment palette, shot list, and ownership record. Existing images are repository-supplied assets; their commercial provenance has not been verified in this implementation.

## Audio direction

Street, chapel, and Ward Nine have distinct musical beds. Scene changes crossfade; music, ambience, and effects have independent controls. The evidence reveal has its own short cue. Sound off stops sources and suspends playback; leaving play also stops the score. Generated beds are reproducible from `scripts/generate-audio.mjs`.

Recorded voice production is a separate human production gate. Lock dialogue, pronunciation, actor permissions, direction, subtitle timing, mix standards, and localization scope before recording. This build includes no commissioned actor recordings and does not present synthesized speech as recorded performances.

## Human playtest protocol

Recruit participants separately with authorization. Start with new players who have not read the README. Observe creation, the chapel, evidence inspection, a witness/archive task, and the final wall. Ask players to describe their goal, the difference between a claim and corroboration, the cost of their disposition, and who was affected by it.

Record confusion points, reading fatigue, missed evidence, choice regret, and whether a second origin changes their approach. Obtain consent before collecting any recordings or participant data. Target at least two revision rounds with fresh participants. No external participants have been recruited or tested by the automated implementation.
