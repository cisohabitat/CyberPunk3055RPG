# Fresh-player slice research protocol

Use this protocol for phases 1–2, then repeat its questions during beta. No recruitment, participant session, or external result has been completed by the repository tools. Obtain separate authorization for outreach and spending. The production review catalog contains spoilers; do not show it before a blind play session.

Invite consented participants with a range of narrative-RPG familiarity and access needs. Explain the corporate violence, coercion, grief, and memory-editing content. Explain the purpose, who sees notes, how long they are kept, that participation can stop, and whether observation or recording is proposed. Obtain recording consent separately. Do not require names, player accounts, or full exported saves. Use a study-local participant code in research notes, not game telemetry.

Record the exact build, device/OS/browser, network, input method, chosen origin, session type, consent status, and facilitator. Collect outcomes only after consent. Keep identifiers and recordings outside the repository under the study’s approved access and retention policy. Publish aggregate findings and redacted evidence references for acceptance.

Ask the participant to begin a run and think aloud only if that is part of the consented protocol. Observe without teaching the rules or steering choices. At natural breaks ask:

1. At the first meeting, have you accepted the job yet? Who owns the hour, what does Quill offer, and what is still unanswered? After accepting, what are you trying to do, who is stopping you, and what did you want the work to buy?
2. What does Mara’s signature establish? What remains uncertain about the later order?
3. What has changed for Nia? Which recording and publication permissions were actually given?
4. Name one consequence you caused, and show where you learned it.
5. After the pump mission, is the circuit repaired, are the medicines safe, and has the later inspection occurred?
6. After Edda’s request, was a wage restored, an appointment booked, or only a form acknowledged? What inquiry still continues?
7. Which origin, skill, item, or trust decision mattered? What would you change on another run?
8. Where did reading become tiring or choices become hard to compare? Rate agency from 1–5 and explain the rating.

Observe voluntary pauses, requests for help, navigation errors, reload recovery, reading adjustments, and incorrect recollections. Separate misunderstood wording from system failures and from intentional ambiguity. Let players stop; do not treat a completed scripted route as comprehension evidence. Record severe blockers with scene/choice ids and a redacted reproduction, then revise before the next cohort.

The full plan proposes two fresh cohorts of roughly 12–20 participants with a revision between them: at least 80% can explain the immediate goal and distinguish testimony from corroboration, at least 75% can identify a personal consequence, median agency at least 4/5, and no recurring blocker. These are provisional design criteria, not market forecasts. Record sample size, recruitment bias, prompted versus unprompted answers, and reasons behind ratings. A small cohort cannot establish percentile performance or a universal review grade.

Store aggregate findings and reviewer decisions as evidence references in `qa/production/acceptance.json` only when the work has occurred. Internal automated campaigns remain a separate evidence category. Carry resulting comprehension, fatigue, accessibility, and economy findings into the next implementation cycle before expanding campaign scale.

For the playable memory timeline, observe whether a fresh player can order the sources without prompting, distinguish contradiction from missing proof, and explain why a correct chronology does not authenticate an issuer or authorize witness publication. Record source-card consultations, draft resets, opted-out chronology, verdict revisions and reading fatigue without treating a scripted successful sequence as comprehension. Ask whether the later trust cost and retained correction feel understandable and proportionate.
