## Phase 2: encounter craft

Prototype an archive investigation and a negotiation that ask different kinds of questions. Retrieval should require interpreting conflicting sources and choosing a practical response. Negotiation should involve a counterpart's concrete want and an offer with a real cost, including a viable refusal. Different builds must change the interaction, preparation or consequence, not merely the success percentage.

**Exit:** two contrasting complete approaches for each anchor, including a scarce/failed alternative. The player can identify the decision, tradeoff and resulting response from the rendered scene. Preserve attribution, authentication limits, once-only costs and affordable recovery. Author the return alongside the encounter; expand only after comparing complete proxy campaigns.

## Phase 3: emotional payoff

Connect the strongest encounter decisions to short present-day returns and the four existing endings. Prefer a person responding or an observable change over another summary of a flag. Keep private words, public testimony, medical care and institutional findings distinct. Unfinished or compromised work must remain honestly unfinished.

**Exit:** contrasting prepared, failed, deferred and unfinished campaigns deliver different earned closing moments. The finale's emotional scene works before optional factual history is expanded. Lock text, actor and art meaning before commissioning or generating performances.

## Phase 4: visual direction

Stage the selected anchor sequences with deliberate establishing views, reactions, expressions and detail shots. Preserve established faces, anatomy and actual scene knowledge. Avoid painting unearned safety, permission or success. Keep the title identity; direct effort toward the ordinary play that follows it.

**Exit:** review whole sequences at phone/desktop/enlarged sizes, including missing-art and text-only delivery. Shots support the current dramatic beat, all visible hands/props and identities are checked, and optional detail controls do not interrupt reading or voices. Existing art is reused where it still fits.

## Phase 5: performance direction

Audition the complete anchor scenes rather than individual file counts. Review emotional intention, emphasis, pronunciation, response spacing and intelligibility over actual beds. Retake weak stock performances where feasible; preserve exact words and character identity. Keep ASR and signal measurements as technical checks, separate from expressive acceptance.

**Exit:** matched actor/text/voice/art, reviewed scene-level before/after performances, natural completion, Stop/mute/manual/zero-channel/interruption behavior and a readable silent version. No runtime synthesis or invented personalized narration is required.

## Phase 6: whole-campaign acceptance

Repeat fixed proxy campaigns spanning origins, builds, resources, failure and the four finales. Assess comprehension, decision fatigue, build recognition and emotional continuity with an unchanged rubric. Keep negative findings and retry evidence. Add physical-device and assistive-technology observations when available; do not relabel synthetic viewports as hardware tests.

**Exit:** resolve repeated severe findings, verify every supported flow and release gate, and publish a new evidence-based assessment. A supported 9+ requires strong craft across the campaign, not a selected impressive scene or a growing asset/test inventory. Further human or independent review can supplement the available AI proxy without blocking ongoing development.
